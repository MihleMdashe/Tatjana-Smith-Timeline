From Olympic glory to a new beginning

2021
27JUL
Silver in 100m breaststroke at Tokyo 2020
 ▾

Won silver in the 100m breaststroke at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics with a time of 1:05.22, finishing behind Lydia Jacoby (USA). She had set an Olympic record of 1:04.82 in the heats.

30JUL
Gold and world record in 200m breaststroke at Tokyo 2020
 ▾

Won gold in the 200m breaststroke at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics in a world record time of 2:18.95, the first individual swimming world record of those Games and South Africa's first gold of Tokyo 2020. She became the first woman to break 2:19 in the event.

After the Tokyo Olympics, her parents emigrated to the Netherlands, where her father was born, leaving Smith to adjust to life without them nearby in South Africa.

2023
28JUL
First world title at Fukuoka World Championships
 ▾

Won her first world title, taking gold in the 200m breaststroke at the Fukuoka World Championships in 2:20.80. This earned her $20 000 in prize money for the gold and $15 000 for the silver, totalling $35 000.

16AUG
SSA withholds $35 000 prize money over kit dispute
 ▾

Swimming South Africa (SSA) informed Smith that her $35 000 in prize money was being withheld, citing a €46 000 fine for wearing her personal sponsor Speedo's kit instead of the federation's official Arena brand. She did wear the Arena cap, the only branded item visible above the waterline. After her legal team questioned the fines, SSA withdrew them, but the prize money was still not paid.

2024
31MAY
First letter of demand to SSA
 ▾

Smith's legal team, Hurter Spies Attorneys, sent an initial letter of demand to SSA regarding the withheld prize money.

29JUL
Gold in 100m breaststroke at Paris 2024
 ▾

Won gold in the 100m breaststroke at the Paris 2024 Olympics in 1:05.28, Africa's first swimming gold of those Games.

1AUG
Silver, retirement, and the interest dispute surfaces
 ▾

Won silver in the 200m breaststroke at the Paris Olympics in 2:19.60, finishing behind Kate Douglass (USA). She then announced her retirement from competitive swimming immediately after the race, ending a 22-year career with four Olympic medals (two gold, two silver), making her South Africa's most decorated Olympian, surpassing Chad le Clos.

SSA operations manager Mafata Modutoane approached her husband Joel Smith that same day and showed proof of payment of the $35 000 prize money, but with no interest included. When Joel questioned the missing interest, Modutoane allegedly replied, “I kept the interest as a management fee.” Modutoane later denied the claim.

2026
JUL
Final letter of demand over withheld interest
 ▾

Smith and her husband approached attorney Wian Spies of Hurter Spies Attorneys to issue a final letter of demand to SSA over the withheld interest.

24AUG
SSA president and CEO suspended by World Aquatics
 ▾

SSA president Alan Fritz and CEO Shaun Adriaanse were provisionally suspended by World Aquatics' Integrity Unit over alleged abuse of power and false documents, deepening the governance crisis at the federation.

25AUG
Formal letter of demand with 10-day ultimatum
 ▾

Hurter Spies Attorneys sent a formal letter of demand to SSA alleging that the federation had “unlawfully held” the $35 000 for more than a year and demanding payment of the accrued interest plus proof of where the funds were held, giving SSA 10 days to comply.

7SEP
PAIA complaint lodged with Information Regulator
 ▾

Smith's legal team lodged a PAIA (Promotion of Access to Information Act) complaint with the Information Regulator, seeking access to the SSA-Arena sponsorship contract, kit policies, and records of fines imposed on athletes. She said she wanted to ensure athletes were not barred from earning income from their own sponsors while swimming in national kit for free.

15SEP
SSA agrees to pay R75 000 interest settlement
 ▾

SSA agreed to pay Smith the full amount of interest claimed on the $35 000, settling at R75 000 in interest. The settlement was publicly confirmed on 16 September 2026 and described by her legal team as a “decisive legal victory.”

21SEP
Announces return to competitive swimming
 ▾

Officially announced her return to competitive swimming, targeting the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics with a new focus on the 50m breaststroke, a newly added Olympic event.

24–27SEP
Competitive comeback at SA Short Course Championships
 ▾

Made her competitive comeback at the South African Short Course Championships in Pietermaritzburg, winning the 50m breaststroke in a personal best of 29.50, the first time she broke 30 seconds in the sprint. She also won relay golds.

22–25OCT
Sweeps all three breaststroke events at Dutch Qualifiers
 ▾

Competed at the Dutch Qualifiers (short course) in Rotterdam, sweeping all three breaststroke events with times of 29.54 (50m), 1:03.92 (100m, just 0.03 off her African record), and 2:19.96 (200m). She represented club Zwemvereniging Westland. Her World Aquatics profile was subsequently updated to list her nationality as NED.

13OCT
SSA formally apologises to Smith in Parliament
 ▾

SSA acting president Zikie Molusi appeared before Parliament's Sport, Arts and Culture Portfolio Committee and formally apologised to Smith for the withheld prize money, saying, “We believe that this issue could have been better handled” and “it shouldn't have happened.” SSA confirmed it had paid back the $35 000 plus R75 000 in interest.

10NOV
Switches sporting nationality to the Netherlands
 ▾

Announced via a tearful Instagram video titled “To my beloved South Africa” that she was switching her sporting nationality from South Africa to the Netherlands, with the goal of competing at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. World Aquatics confirmed the nationality change. She cited “financial and practical challenges of swimming” and the need for “greater high performance support and the potential to build a more sustainable career.” She said she would continue to live and train in South Africa and remain committed to her non-profit helping young swimmers.

Sports Minister Gayton McKenzie responded, “Every medal, every record and every anthem she gave us belongs to our history forever. We now have to share her with the Netherlands.”

1–6DEC
Set to represent the Netherlands at World Short Course Championships
 ▾

Set to represent the Netherlands for the first time internationally at the 2026 World Short Course Swimming Championships in Beijing.
